# Maximalism and expressive design

## Traits

- "More is more", the reverse of minimalism: saturated palettes with several strong colors,
  large and expressive type (variable weights, outlined, stretched, overlapping), layered
  patterns, stickers, shapes, collage and photography together.
- **Expressive design** is the controlled version: bold color, varied shape, size contrast,
  motion and containment used on purpose to point at what matters and to create emotion,
  within a clear system.
- **Chaos / Gen Z maximalism** is the loose version: collage, mixed fonts, stickers,
  deliberately messy composition; close to antidesign when overdone.

## Evidence: Material 3 Expressive

Google's design team reports 46 studies with over 18,000 participants over about three years
behind Material 3 Expressive (announced May 2025). Reported findings: expressive designs were
preferred across ages, most strongly by 18-24-year-olds (up to 87%); participants found key
elements up to four times faster; ratings rose for modern, energetic and friendly attributes;
older users spotted interactive elements as well as younger users. The same report warns that
expressiveness must not break established patterns: a playlist redesign with scattered album
art looked modern but failed usability tests, and domains such as banking should keep their
conventions. This is vendor research without a published method, so the numbers are
indicative, not independent evidence.

The useful lesson: expressive tactics work when they increase hierarchy (the important thing
gets bigger, bolder, more colorful), not when they add decoration everywhere.

## Fits

- Youth, music, fashion, festival, entertainment and creator brands.
- Campaign and launch pages, portfolios of illustrators and art directors.
- Consumer apps that want personality, using the expressive (systematic) version.

## Do not use when

- Trust-first, task-critical or dense-data products (banking, health, government, B2B
  dashboards), beyond one expressive accent such as a hero or an empty state.
- The audience is general and the loose chaos version is proposed: NN/g's antidesign warning
  applies; users need clear hierarchy to act.
- The team cannot keep a system; maximalism without rules becomes noise quickly.

## Accessibility and performance

- Many saturated colors raise the chance that text-on-color pairs fail 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.2 SC
  1.4.3); check every combination actually used, not just the main one.
- Overlapping text and images harm reading and screen magnifier users.
- Big variable fonts and layered images add weight; subset fonts and compress assets.
- Color must not be the only way information is shown.

## Keep it usable

- Loud surface, calm structure: keep grid, navigation placement, form patterns and body text
  conventional.
- Use expressive tactics to rank content: the primary action gets the strongest color, shape
  and size; limit the number of "loud" elements per screen so hierarchy survives (NN/g's
  scale principle: few distinct sizes).
- Define the system: a palette with roles, two or three type styles for display, a pattern and
  shape library, and motion rules.
- Keep one quiet zone for reading (a plain panel for body copy).

## Mixing

- Expressive color and type over a flat 2.0 base (this is what Material 3 Expressive does).
- Maximalism pairs with retro eras (Y2K, Memphis), neobrutalism and kinetic typography.

## Trade-offs

- Attention, memorability and appeal to young audiences against a narrower fit, higher design
  effort and faster fatigue for frequent users.

## Common mistakes

- Every element loud, so nothing is prominent.
- Five display fonts on one page.
- Expressive shapes on standard controls that users no longer recognise as buttons or inputs.
